Critical Analysis 5: Face, Politeness and Declining Invitations

Abstract

An analysis of how participants decline an invitation without offending - managing positive and negative face through accounts, counteroffers, filled pauses and silence - and how third-party criticism is softened and repaired among friends.

Henry starts with a pre-proposal that Diana has not visited their new unit, showing interest and orienting to her positive face. Diana responds (line 2) with an apology, expressing accountability for not making enough effort, and shows sincere interest to see the new unit to fulfill the social expectation (Mitchel & Haugh, 2015). The acknowledgment-token ah (line 4) and proposal of inviting Diana over for dinner, with a tag question (line 5) is a strategy to avoid imposing on Diana's negative face and showing consideration for Diana's positive face (Labben, 2018). In line 6, there is an inter-turn silence, which means a dispreferred response will follow, as preferred responses do not have a gap. I-I think (line 7) suggests a problematic topic as Diana declines the invitation and gives an account with a reason that her mum has invited someone to save face (Mitchell & Haugh, 2015). Emma provides a counteroffer (line 9) after an inter-turn silence (repair initiator) that Diana should come home with them tonight instead, providing another proposal to save face. In line 10, Diana responds with well erm, a filled pause that indicates another dispreferred response (Labben, 2018). She gives another account providing information about her ill grandfather to indirectly decline to save face. Emma is assessing the situation by challenging Diana’s decline (line 12), as Diana recently visited her grandfather, threatening her negative face (Grainger, 2018), to which Diana accepts to save face (preferred response). In line 14, Emma uses a bald-on-record strategy that Diana is coming with them tonight, this is to avoid contradicting Diana's explanation (Grainger, 2018). The erm pause and okay pause (line 15) is a shift-implicative usually followed by rejection (Mitchell & Haugh, 2015). Diana gives an account that she needs to let her mum know, this mitigation is a way of minimising face-threatening act (FTA) of refusing the invitation again and gives a reason that her decision to accept or refuse is not entirely up to her as her mother has authority, saving negative face (Konokahara, 2017). The inter-turn silence (line 16) shows the end of the sequence.

Henry starts a new sequence (line 17) with an informing-implicative because he wants the others to respond. Emma displays interest (preferred response) and requests more information, which allows Henry (line 19) to share his other-criticism about Robert spending thousands of pounds on his playroom (Mitchell & Haugh, 2015). This is an unexpected answer as Diana gives an account (other-challenge) that Robert's sister was in financial trouble, FTA orienting to Henry’s negative face (line 21). This makes Henry accountable, and he gives an explanation using reported speech, that Robert said his sister could manage, to save his positive face while validating his other-criticism (Konokahara, 2017). Emma (line 25) aligns herself with Henry by giving an account orienting to support his observations with another other-criticism about Robert, this allows Henry to escalate (line 26). Diana gives a side sequence (line 27) showing offence to Henry's negative assessment of rich Brits, which is also directed at her as she associates herself with that group (Mitchell & Haugh, 2015). Both Henry and Emma (lines 28-29) try to save face (initiating repair) by reassuring Diana they are not associating her with Robert, affirming her positive face (Mitchell & Haugh, 2015).
